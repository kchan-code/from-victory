## Clinical routing memo — the withheld cells (for the advisor when seated)

The map withheld the four shootout cells on **structural** grounds: "every hard-moment cell in this app ends on *next rep*, and the shootout has no next rep." That objection is real but it is **solvable**, and I want it retired so it does not get re-litigated at render time.

**The shootout has no next *rep*. It has a next *action*.** The Return step of Name → Reject → Reset → Return has never actually required a repetition of the failed skill — it requires that attention move onto a concrete, immediately-available, athlete-controlled action. Soccer supplies four of them, all specific, all runnable inside thirty seconds, none of them a kick:

1. **The walk back.** A hundred yards of it, chosen posture, in full view. It is the single most physically concrete "next action" in the entire soccer track — more concrete than a backcheck.
2. **Re-entering the line.** The team stands linked at the center circle. Putting your arm back around the two players next to you is a physical re-entry into the group that the miss told you you had forfeited. That is the reset cue, and it does exactly the psychological work the cell needs.
3. **Being there for the next taker.** If you are not the last kick, the shootout continues and there are teammates still to walk past you.
4. **Going to the keeper (or, for the keeper, to the ones who missed).** After the final kick the losing keeper is alone in the goal. Someone reaches them first. That is an action, it is available, and it is the opposite of the isolation the miss produces.

So the reset cue for these cells is **postural and social rather than motor** — head up, turn before you want to, walk, rejoin, reach someone. That is a legitimate Return beat, it is bench-runnable in the strict sense the mental-skill spec requires, and it does not fake a rep that does not exist.

**My recommendation nonetheless: the withhold stands — on stronger grounds than the architectural one.**

The architectural objection dissolves. A **clinical/performance** objection replaces it, and an advisor should be shown this specifically:

- **This is the one cell in the product where rehearsing the failure may help produce it.** Every other HM cell rehearses an adversity that (a) is near-certain to occur in some form and (b) precedes dozens of subsequent reps, so the preparedness benefit of coping imagery dominates. A penalty kick is a discrete, closed, high-attention skill with a single execution. Detailed pre-event imagery of the ball going over the bar is exactly the wrong pre-shot content for it, and the athlete may be selected to take one within hours of hearing the clip. Coping imagery is normally protective; here the temporal adjacency inverts the risk profile.
- **It contradicts a clip we already ship.** `viz-soc-fwd-penalty` and `viz-soc-gk-penalty-save` rehearse the setup, run-up, strike, and save. Putting a miss-rehearsal in the same 5-minute session is incoherent coaching.
- **The aftermath is grief-adjacent and runs past what a pregame session can hold.** The silence, the scapegoating, the group chat, the car — those are real, and a 5-minute audio that opens them and then hands the athlete to a send-off prayer has opened something it cannot close. The map's own instinct (§4, §9, §10.5) — **postgame / "For the Ride Home"** — is the right surface. That is where these four drafts should ultimately migrate, re-cut for the postgame shape.
- **The in-run-of-play missed penalty already ships** as a reading of `I miss a big chance.` The athlete who wants penalty-pressure work is served.

So: authored, complete, structurally sound, and **still withheld** — now for a reason a clinician can act on rather than one an engineer can argue with.

Two standing notes for the advisor on all five: line 3 in these cells carries an **identity verdict** as the quoted thought (`I'm not a taker`, `the one thing I'm for`, `I'm supposed to win this for them`). That is a deliberate FV-412 exception — per the style spec's Open KC decision item 8, in gated cells the identity lie *is* the clinical subject, and the cell exists to defuse it, not to install it. Every one of these is a lie the athlete has already heard in their own head; none is composed for them. Confirm that judgment. And none of the five names the collapse ("the yips"), per the books.

### Per-cell clinical design notes (for the advisor)


**Cell 1.**The forward's wound is **role fusion**: "you're only as good as your last goal" is said out loud, by adults, to sixteen-year-olds (map §4), and the penalty is the purest possible test of the one thing the position is publicly for. So line 3 voices the fusion rather than a behavioral read — defusing it is the cell's whole job. Line 5 does two things in order: an honest **arousal reappraisal for post-event arousal** (the surge is caring with nowhere to go, and it is time-limited — not evidence of damage, and explicitly *not* reframed as "fuel," which would be a lie here), then a strict **event-level naming** — "a penalty went over the bar" — that refuses the verdict without arguing with it. Line 6 relocates the Return beat from motor to postural: turning early, head up, and rejoining the linked line is a controllable, 30-second, physically real action that directly contradicts the exile the miss manufactures. Line 7 is plain and pre-dated — worth established *before* the kick, so nothing on the spot could have been carrying it.


**Cell 2.**The midfielder's locus is **duty and reliability** — "the engine," the one who is trusted to do the job — so this variant is written around *volunteering*, which is the truest midfield version of the moment and produces a distinct wound: not "I failed at what I'm for" but "I chose this and it cost them." Line 3 is a behavioral read (`I asked for that one`), FV-412-compliant on its face, which makes this the least clinically loaded of the four. Line 5 is the cell's medicine and is straight process-over-outcome: penalty conversion is not under the taker's control in any meaningful sense, but **stepping forward is**, and it is the piece the athlete actually executed. Naming that is honest rather than consoling. Line 6 gives the midfielder the Return beat that matches the position's identity — the connector, the voice — and, critically, it is available *because the shootout is still running*: there are teammates still to walk past. Line 7 refuses both directions of the transaction: the courage did not buy worth, the miss did not spend it.


**Cell 3.**The defender's shootout is the **imposter** version — the athlete who does not take penalties, taken anyway because the list ran out, which is exactly how the fifth kick gets filled in real youth and college soccer. So line 3 carries the avoidance lie (`I never should have been up there`) rather than a role-failure lie; the collapse being defused is *"I was exposed as not belonging in that moment,"* which for the position whose identity is "nothing gets past me" is a specific and recognizable inversion. Line 5 deliberately anchors the outcome to something the athlete plainly did not control — the width of a post — while affirming the part they did: consenting to the kick. That is a Dweck-shaped effort/choice attribution, and it is factually true rather than consoling, which matters for this audience's bullshit detector. Line 6 is the strongest Return beat in the four: after a final missed kick the opposing keeper's counterpart is alone in the goal, and the defender — last line, the keeper's protector all match — is the natural person to reach them. It converts the athlete from the object of the moment into an agent inside it, without redemption framing. Line 7 states worth as **pre-existing and unchanged**, in the plainest register the books allow.


**Cell 4.**The keeper's shootout wound is categorically different from the outfield three and is authored as such: not *I failed at my job* but **I was cast as the one who wins this, and I wasn't.** That is a hero-role expectation the athlete did not agree to and cannot meet reliably, which is why line 3 voices the expectation itself rather than a performance verdict. Line 5 is the only place in the five where I use a base-rate correction, and it is used carefully: penalties overwhelmingly favor the shooter, so *not saving* is the modal outcome and is not an error — but the line refuses the easy dismissal ("it wasn't on you," which this audience reads as a lie) by naming a real process win instead: reading two of five correctly is competent goalkeeping, evaluated on the part the keeper controls (set, read, drive) rather than the part they don't. That is Bandura-style mastery attribution built from process evidence, not from outcome or praise. Line 6 gives the keeper the one Return action nobody else in the sport has: they are the only person on the field who has just lived the same walk the missers took, which makes reaching them first both concrete and uniquely theirs. Line 7 severs worth from the save ledger in both directions in a single plain sentence — the shortest line 7 of the set, on purpose, because this is the heaviest cell.


**Cell 5.**Modeled directly on the shipped `hm-bsb-catcher-lose-command` register: **event-scoped, time-scoped, mechanism-named, worth-truth line 7.** Line 2 shows the actual progression a keeper coach would recognize — a ball through the hands, a second one, then the behavioral consequence that matters most (staying on the line, refusing the cross) — because the withdrawal, not the drop, is the thing that costs goals and generalizes. Line 3's thought is deliberately **tonight-scoped** (`my hands are gone tonight`, mirroring baseball's `my throw is gone tonight`) rather than permanent; keeping the temporal boundary inside the quoted thought is the main protective device in a motor-anxiety cell, and it should survive any advisor re-cut. Line 5 gives a real mechanism — arousal produces early, hard hands, and hard hands catch late — because for motor-anxiety an accurate physiological explanation reduces threat appraisal far more than reassurance does; then it counts the actual events (two), against the actual history (ten thousand), and stops. Line 6 names the position's isolation **honestly and then uses it**: there is no substitution pattern and nowhere to hide, so the demand is lowered rather than the athlete told to be tough — one ball, body behind it, the easiest available (a back-pass, a goal kick), which is a genuine mastery-experience ladder rather than "trust your hands." The cross is deliberately *not* prescribed as the next action; asking a keeper mid-collapse to come and claim in traffic is asking them to fail. Line 7 follows the baseball cadence to its "free" close because free hands are literally the therapeutic target.

**Standing guardrails verified across all five:** no head-impact or playing-on-after-contact content; no body-composition or leanness content; no gendered duel language (no he/him — "the keeper," "the taker," "your team"); US register (field, gloves, center circle); no British slang; the collapse is never named; no comparison to another athlete; no outcome-contingent confidence; no shame framing. If an athlete's response to any of these five moves toward persistent hopelessness, it routes to Option C (988, Crisis Text Line, trusted adult), not to more content.
